Roots can find their way into joints and cracks in underground lines. Cutting the growth clears the obstruction, while an inspection helps show whether the pipe itself also needs repair.

What is happening inside the line?

Roots can enter a private service line even when the public sewer is fine, because the two are separate pieces of pipe. A camera inspection shows where roots have entered, so the cleaning plan matches the actual line.

How hydro jetting may help

Specialized jetting nozzles can break up and flush root growth in a pipe that is safe to clean. A camera check is important because roots often point to a joint opening or a broken section; water alone will not seal that entry point. A qualified professional can explain whether spot repair or replacement should follow cleaning.

When to ask for a closer look

Slow drainage across several fixtures or a recurring main-line backup may suggest a root problem, but those symptoms do not identify its cause by themselves. Have the line checked before treating the blockage. Avoid assuming every root-filled line is a candidate for high-pressure cleaning.

Hudson considerations

Hudson’s historic residential areas include older homes, but a home’s age does not date its service line. Ask where roots entered and whether the joint can stay sound after cleaning. Jetting cannot close the opening that admitted the roots.

Questions about the sewer line go to the Hudson sewer provider, and the city's permit guidance explains when a permit applies.

Common questions

Will hydro jetting stop tree roots permanently?

Jetting can remove an existing root obstruction, but it does not close the gap that let roots enter. An inspection may show that pipe repair is needed to reduce recurrence.

Can roots mean the sewer pipe is cracked?

Roots can enter through gaps or damaged joints, though their presence alone does not show the extent of damage. A camera inspection can help locate the entry point and guide the next step.
